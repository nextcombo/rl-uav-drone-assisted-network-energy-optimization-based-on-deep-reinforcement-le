Abstract: This paper investigates the energy efficiency (EE) optimization problem for UAVs providing wireless coverage to both static and mobile ground users. Recent multi-agent reinforcement learning methods optimize system EE through two-dimensional trajectory design, but do not consider interference from neighboring UAV cellular networks. The objective of this study is to achieve maximum system EE by jointly optimizing the three-dimensional trajectories of each UAV, the number of connected users, and energy consumption while addressing interference issues. To this end, we propose a collaborative multi-agent distributed dual deep Q-network (MAD-DDQN) approach. This method outperforms existing benchmark methods in terms of EE by up to 55%-80%.
Keywords: Energy Efficiency; UAV Base Station; Deep Reinforcement Learning; Multi-Agent System
I. Introduction
The deployment of drones for providing wireless coverage to ground users has received extensive research attention [1]-[7]. Drones can provide connectivity to a large number of static or mobile devices, playing a critical role in supporting Internet of Things (IoT) networks [1]. More importantly, drones have numerous practical applications, ranging from disaster assistance communication to surveillance and search and rescue operations [8],[9]. Specifically, in scenarios where network congestion or existing ground infrastructure is unavailable, drones can be deployed to provide services. However, to offer comprehensive services to dynamic ground users, drones need sophisticated strategies to optimize flight trajectories while ensuring coverage quality.
The study of multi-drone network energy efficiency optimization has achieved significant results in recent years [1]-[5]. However, these studies rely on ground central controllers for drone decision-making, which is difficult to implement in emergency scenarios due to the need for extensive information exchange between drones and controllers. Additionally, tracking user locations becomes challenging in such scenarios.
Machine learning is gradually being used to address complex deployment issues of multiple drones, with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods being employed in several studies to optimize system energy efficiency. The distributed Q-learning method proposed in [1] focuses on optimizing drone energy usage but does not consider overall system energy efficiency; this challenge can be addressed by Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods proposed in [7]. In our previous research [10], a deep reinforcement learning-based approach was proposed to optimize the energy efficiency of fixed-wing drones - these drones typically cannot hover like rotary-wing drones and the study only focused on scenarios where coverage is provided to static ground users.
The distributed deep reinforcement learning method proposed in [3] is an improvement over the centralized method in [5], where a single autonomous agent controls all drones. Both [3] and [5] propose the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) method, allowing drones to hover at a fixed height to provide coverage to static ground users in an uninterrupted network environment, thereby enhancing system energy efficiency. Although these two methods are expected to improve performance in coverage scoring, they only optimize the two-dimensional trajectories of drones serving static ground users.
The study focuses on maximizing system energy efficiency by optimizing the three-dimensional trajectories of each drone within a series of time steps, taking into account the impact of neighboring drone clusters and the coverage for both static and moving ground users. We propose a collaborative multi-agent distributed dual deep Q-network (MAD-DDQN) approach where the reward for each agent reflects its coverage performance in its surrounding area. This method achieves maximum system energy efficiency without compromising network performance.

This section will construct the problem model and propose the MAD-DDQN algorithm, which optimizes the trajectory of each drone to maximize the overall system energy efficiency.

The computational complexity of problem (7a) is NP-complete [6], and it is a non-convex problem with multiple local optima. Thus, traditional optimization methods face significant challenges in solving problem (7a) [1], [6]. Especially when more drones are deployed in a shared wireless environment, problem (7a) becomes more complex, making it difficult to find an optimal cooperative strategy that not only improves system energy efficiency but also fulfills coverage tasks in dynamic scenarios.
This is often because drones may exhibit "selfishness"—prioritizing their own energy efficiency, minimizing communication interruptions and energy consumption over collective goals of maximizing system energy efficiency. In such cases, when individual interests conflict with collective interests, cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning methods are more suitable. Deep reinforcement learning has been shown to perform well in decision-making tasks in dynamic environments [14], hence we adopt cooperative deep multi-agent reinforcement learning to solve the problem of system energy efficiency optimization.

(III) DDQN implementation as shown in Figure 2, the neural network (NN) architecture of the agent j for DDQN includes a 5-dimensional state space input vector connected to two fully connected layers with 128 and 64 nodes respectively, each layer using ReLU activation function, and finally outputs an 7-dimensional output layer. Our distributed approach assumes that the agents are independent learners.
According to the analysis in [15], the neural network architecture used in MAD-DDQN has a computational complexity of approximately O (D_s K W), with an average response time of 5.6ms. In contrast, the most relevant research and evaluation benchmark, MADDPG, has a computational complexity of approximately O (D_s K W) + O ((D_a + D_s) K W), with an average response time of 7.4ms. Here, D_s represents the dimensionality of the state space, D_a denotes the dimensionality of the action space, K indicates the number of layers, and W is the number of nodes per hidden layer.
In the training phase, agent j takes state information as input to train the main network to generate better decisions - outputting Q-values for each possible action, with the maximum Q-value determining the action taken by the agent. At each time step, agent j observes the current state s and updates its trajectory based on its policy by selecting an action a. After taking an action at time step t, agent j receives a reward r according to equation (8) and transitions to a new state s'. Subsequently, the information (s, a, r, s') is input into the experience replay buffer (as shown in Figure 2).
Through multiple rounds of training, the Q-network is optimized using the ε-greedy strategy to balance exploration and exploitation [16]. In the ε-greedy strategy, actions are selected randomly with probability ε and with probability 1-ε, choosing the action with the highest value. The initial value of ε is set to 1, which is linearly decreased to 0.01.

We simulated 2-12 unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in a 1000×1000 m² area serving static and mobile ground users (as shown in Figure 2), performing 2000 Monte Carlo (MC) trials during training rounds. Figure 3 compares the MAD-DDQN method with the benchmark method, analyzing the impact of UAV deployment number on energy efficiency, ground user interruption rate, and total energy consumption. Since the baseline method MADDPG converged (learned appropriate behavior) significantly longer, to ensure fairness in comparison, the training rounds for the MAD-DDQN method were set to 250 rounds, while MADDPG was conducted over 2000 rounds.
Due to our focus on comparing the energy efficiency values rather than presenting their absolute values, we normalize the energy efficiency values based on the mean of the MAD-DDQN method. As shown in Figure 3a, the performance of the MAD-DDQN method consistently outperforms both the random strategy and the MADDPG method across all drone deployment counts, with improvements of approximately 80% and 55%, respectively.
Interestingly, as shown in Figure 3b, in terms of minimizing ground user interruptions, the performance of the MADDPG method only exceeds that of the MAD-DDQN method by about 2%, but this slight performance gain comes at a cost of training computational costs — the training cost for the MADDPG method is eight times that of the MAD-DDQN method. Intuitively, the MAD-DDQN method reduces the need for experience by discretizing the agent's action space, which hides redundant information in the environment, resulting in less learning required compared to the MADDPG method. By contrast, the random strategy performs worst in reducing connection interruptions, highlighting the importance of strategic decision-making in multi-agent reinforcement learning problems.
Figure 3c clearly shows that compared to the baseline methods, the proposed methods significantly reduce the total energy consumption of all drones. Although the MADDPG method slightly outperforms the proposed method in reducing interruptions, the MAD-DDQN method has significantly higher energy efficiency, meaning that the MADDPG method trades off energy consumption for improved ground user coverage quality.
Figure 4 demonstrates the change in energy efficiency over learning rounds (by varying the number of agents) to verify the convergence of the MAD-DDQN method. We observe that as the number of drones increases, the converged value of energy efficiency steadily declines — this is because more drones lead to system stability reduction, increased interference, and thus a decrease in system throughput. Overall, regardless of how many drones are deployed in the network, the collaborative MAD-DDQN method can achieve system energy efficiency convergence.
V. Conclusion This paper proposes the MAD-DDQN method for optimizing the energy efficiency of a drone fleet serving static and mobile ground users in constrained environments. The method ensures that agents quickly adapt and converge, enabling them to learn strategies that maximize system total energy efficiency under strict energy budget constraints by jointly optimizing three-dimensional trajectories, the number of connected users, and energy consumption. Extensive simulation results show that, without degrading network coverage performance, the MAD-DDQN method significantly outperforms random strategies and state-of-the-art distributed multi-agent reinforcement learning methods in terms of energy efficiency.
